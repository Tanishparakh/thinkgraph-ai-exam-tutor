# Thinking Skills Tutor Platform Research

Research date: 13 June 2026

## Research Scope

This review examined public product, course, sample, pricing, and help pages from
Test Academy, SelectiveGuru, Cortex Academy/Cortex Online, Elevated Tuition, and
Vision Academics. It also checked official NSW test guidance, the Cambridge
Thinking Skills syllabus, and evidence-based teaching guidance.

No private accounts, paid content, copied questions, or proprietary category
lists were used.

## Verified Platform Patterns

### Test Academy

- Connects four learning modes: AI coaching, adaptive skill practice, realistic
  timed mock tests, and performance analytics.
- Lets students practise a chosen skill in timed or untimed mode.
- Adjusts question difficulty and gives a step-by-step AI explanation.
- Tracks scores, trends, time per question, recent attempts, and weak areas.
- Produces a weekly plain-English recommendation about what to focus on.

Product lesson: explanations should feed directly into the next targeted task.

### SelectiveGuru

- Uses short daily practice sets and a personalised study plan.
- Matches explanations to the exact wrong option selected by the student.
- Saves mistakes for reattempt, offers similar-question practice, and tracks
  mastery.
- Breaks parent analytics down to topic and subtopic level.
- Emphasises consistency, misconception diagnosis, and screen stamina.

Product lesson: feedback should explain the student's misconception, not merely
repeat the worked solution.

### Cortex Academy / Cortex Online

- Uses a structured syllabus across Critical, Logical, Abstract, and Numerical
  Reasoning.
- Progresses through theory videos, skill-building exercises, topic tests, and
  exam simulation.
- Provides a large question bank, repeated attempts, detailed answers, and
  timing/accuracy analytics.

Product lesson: students need an explicit curriculum and theory-first support,
not an unstructured stream of random questions.

### Elevated Tuition

- Uses interactive, student-driven teaching with real-time feedback.
- Teaches the correct strategy before asking students to practise repeatedly.
- Supplies targeted additional worksheets when a student needs more practice.
- Focuses on methods that remain usable under exam pressure.

Product lesson: every question type needs a short, reusable method and a clear
practice prescription.

### Vision Academics

- Starts with diagnostic assessment and identifies strengths and weaknesses.
- Creates a personalised learning plan.
- Uses weekly lessons, homework, exam-style practice, progress tracking, and
  strategy refinement.
- Prefers step-by-step methods over undirected question volume.

Product lesson: progress data should lead to a simple learning plan.

## Evidence-Based Teaching Implications

- Official NSW guidance confirms that Thinking Skills combines critical
  thinking and problem solving under strict one-question-per-minute conditions.
- Cambridge organises Thinking Skills around problem solving and critical
  thinking, including understanding, using, analysing, and communicating
  information, plus evaluating evidence and reasoning.
- Australian evidence-based teaching guidance recommends explicit instruction,
  sequencing small amounts of new information, worked examples for novices, and
  more independent problem solving as expertise grows.
- Metacognitive guidance recommends teaching students to plan, monitor, and
  evaluate the strategy they use.
- Effective feedback should close the gap between current and desired
  performance with a clear next action.

## ThinkGraph Product Decision

ThinkGraph should use this learning loop:

1. Detect the domain, category, and precise subskill.
2. State a short reusable strategy before the worked solution.
3. Solve the question in visible, checkable steps.
4. Explain every option.
5. Diagnose the selected distractor and name the mistake pattern.
6. Recommend one next subskill and generate one original practice question.
7. Store accuracy and mistake trends locally.
8. Use those trends to identify the weakest category and recommended focus.

The student-facing interface remains simple. Auto Detect is the default.
Optional advanced settings allow domain and category overrides, while the
60-subskill taxonomy remains internal.
